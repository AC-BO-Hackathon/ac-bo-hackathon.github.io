### Hackathon Project Continuations

Several hackathon projects evolved directly into peer-reviewed publications. The most striking example is Andy Anker's ScattBO scattering-data benchmark, which provided the methodological foundation for his subsequent work on autonomous synthesis of nanoparticles with target scattering patterns, published in ACS Nano in 2026 (baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 1-2). The "Bayesian Optimization for Generality" project, co-led by Strieth-Kalthoff, Haddadnia, Schmid, Kang, Rajaonson, and Park, matured into the BoTier software library for hierarchical multi-objective Bayesian optimization with tiered objective structures, published in Digital Discovery in 2025 (haddadnia2025botiermultiobjectivebayesian pages 1-2, haddadnia2025botiermultiobjectivebayesian pages 5-6). Rubén Laplaza's hackathon project on adaptive batch sizes for BO of reaction yields is thematically linked to his subsequent cost-informed Bayesian reaction optimization (CIBO) paper, which introduces cost-awareness into the BO decision loop for chemical experimentation (schoepfer2024costinformedbayesianreaction pages 1-2). Farshud Sorourifar's benchmarking MolDAIS project foreshadowed his continued work on multi-objective Bayesian optimization for de novo molecular design, culminating in a generative MOBO framework published in Industrial & Engineering Chemistry Research in 2026 (muthyala2025generativemultiobjectivebayesian pages 1-5). Taicheng Guo's hackathon project on optimizing chemical reaction conditions with multi-agent systems connected to his broader research program on LLM-based multi-agent systems, including a widely-cited survey and the ChemOrch framework for empowering LLMs with chemical intelligence (baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 1-2).

### Affiliation Transitions

Among the early-career cohort, several notable affiliation transitions were detected. Magdalena Lederbauer moved from ETH Zurich/EPFL to Entalpic, a Paris-based AI-for-chemistry startup, where she led the development of LeMat-Synth, a multi-modal toolbox for curating synthesis procedure databases (lederbauer2025lematsynthamultimodal pages 1-3). Shi Xuan Leong transitioned from the Department of Chemistry at the University of Toronto to Nanyang Technological University in Singapore, as evidenced by her affiliation in the 2025 Faraday Discussions Spiers Memorial Lecture (cheng2025spiersmemoriallecture pages 1-3). Madhav Reddy Muthyala moved from The Ohio State University to the University of Wisconsin–Madison, appearing as first author on a generative multi-objective BO paper from his new institution (muthyala2025generativemultiobjectivebayesian pages 1-5). Yunheng Zou moved from the University of Waterloo to the University of Toronto / Acceleration Consortium, where he became first author of El Agente, an autonomous agent for quantum chemistry published in Matter (zou2025elagentean pages 1-3). Andy Anker's affiliation evolved from DTU (Department of Energy Conversion and Storage, Technical University of Denmark) to a dual appointment at the Technical University of Copenhagen and Oxford University (baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 1-2).

### Overall Trends for the Early-Career Cohort

The post-hackathon trajectories of these early-career researchers reveal several notable patterns. First, there is a strong tendency toward continued engagement with Bayesian optimization and related methods in chemistry—at least 20 of the 27 authors with traceable activity published directly in the BO/active learning/SDL space within two years of the hackathon. This suggests the event served not merely as a networking exercise but as a genuine catalyst for research directions. The hackathon-to-publication pipeline was particularly effective for projects that addressed concrete methodological gaps (ScattBO, BoTier, CIBO, generative MOBO), where the sprint format produced working prototypes that could be scaled into full papers.

Second, the affiliation transitions reveal a pattern consistent with normal early-career mobility, but with a notable clustering around the AI-for-chemistry ecosystem. Moves from academic postdoc positions to startups (Lederbauer to Entalpic), cross-continental academic moves (Leong to NTU Singapore), and transitions from one research group to another within the BO/SDL community (Zou from Waterloo to the Acceleration Consortium at U of Toronto) suggest that the hackathon community itself may function as a talent pipeline for institutions investing in autonomous experimentation.

Third, the University of Toronto / Acceleration Consortium cluster (Tom, Ser, Pablo-García, Wang, Zou, Yakavets, Huang, Kang) remained a prolific hub, producing major outputs including the Atlas SDL brain software (hickman2025atlasabrain pages 1-2), El Agente autonomous quantum chemistry agent (zou2025elagentean pages 1-3), and the Spiers Memorial Lecture on impactful AI research for chemistry (cheng2025spiersmemoriallecture pages 1-3). The EPFL/ETH Zurich cluster (Laplaza, Wellawatte, Bran, Schwaller, Lederbauer, Schmid) similarly maintained high productivity, with outputs spanning cost-informed BO, explainable AI for chemistry, and LLM-chemistry agent frameworks.

Fourth, a significant fraction of hackathon participants also participated in the 2024 LLM Hackathon for Applications in Materials Science and Chemistry (Zimmermann et al. 2024), suggesting that this cohort is forming a durable, cross-event community. The overlap includes Afzal, Ai, Al-Feghali, Ansari, Borisova, Bran, Fisicaro, Issa, Lederbauer, Plessers, Schwaller, Sparks, Sudhakar, Shi, and others, indicating that hackathon-style community science is becoming a recurring feature of career development in this field (baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 1-2).

Finally, it should be noted that many co-authors—particularly those from industry (Merck KGaA staff such as Hoche, Hopp, Li, Rihana, Plötz, Šošić, Fitzner) and those from less research-intensive institutions—were not found to have post-hackathon publications in the BO/AL/SDL domain during the search window. This is expected: industry researchers often publish less frequently, and the hackathon may have served different functions (skill-building, networking) for participants at different career stages.
