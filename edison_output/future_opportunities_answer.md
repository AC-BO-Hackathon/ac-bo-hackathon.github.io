## 1. BO for Autonomous / Self-Driving Laboratories

Self-driving laboratories (SDLs) integrate robotics, automation, and machine learning to close the loop between experimental design, execution, and analysis, enabling autonomous scientific discovery (abolhasani2023theriseof pages 2-3, abolhasani2023theriseof pages 1-2). BO serves as the predominant "brain" of SDLs, guiding experiment selection to maximize information gain while minimizing resource consumption. SDLs have demonstrated remarkable success across domains including nanomaterials synthesis, thin-film optimization, and electrocatalyst discovery, with the potential to reduce materials development timelines from 10–20 years to 1–2 years (hao2025autonomousmaterialssynthesis pages 1-4). Future opportunities include improving standardized hardware/software integration, developing more adaptive BO algorithms for dynamic experimental environments, and scaling SDL platforms for broader accessibility (formica2025bridginginnovationand pages 2-3, formica2025bridginginnovationand pages 3-4).

**Key references:**
- Tom, G. *et al.* "Self-Driving Laboratories for Chemistry and Materials Science," *Chemical Reviews* **124**(16), 9633–9732 (2024). DOI: [10.1021/acs.chemrev.4c00055](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemrev.4c00055)
- Abolhasani, M. & Kumacheva, E. "The rise of self-driving labs in chemical and materials sciences," *Nature Synthesis* **2**, 483–492 (2023). DOI: [10.1038/s44160-022-00231-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44160-022-00231-0)

## 2. Multi-Fidelity BO

Multi-fidelity Bayesian optimization (MFBO) leverages data from information sources of varying accuracy and cost—such as semi-empirical calculations, density functional theory, and laboratory experiments—to accelerate optimization while reducing total expenditure (judge2024applyingmultifidelitybayesian pages 1-3, sabanzagil2025bestpracticesfor pages 1-3). Best practices established by Sabanza-Gil et al. recommend that the low-fidelity source should be both highly informative and low-cost, with an optimal low-fidelity-to-high-fidelity query ratio of approximately 5:1 and high-fidelity queries kept below 40% of total calls (sabanzagil2025bestpracticesfor pages 9-12). Key open challenges include selecting optimal acquisition functions (e.g., multi-fidelity expected improvement vs. knowledge gradient), understanding cost–fidelity trade-offs, and extending MFBO to multi-objective and constrained settings in chemistry (judge2024applyingmultifidelitybayesian pages 1-3, arroyave2022aperspectiveon pages 6-7).

**Key references:**
- Sabanza-Gil, V. *et al.* "Best practices for multi-fidelity Bayesian optimization in materials and molecular research," *Nature Computational Science* **5**(7), 572–581 (2025). DOI: [10.48550/arxiv.2410.00544](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2410.00544)
- Judge, E. *et al.* "Applying Multi-Fidelity Bayesian Optimization in Chemistry: Open Challenges and Major Considerations," *arXiv* (2024). DOI: [10.48550/arxiv.2409.07190](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2409.07190)

## 3. Uncertainty-Aware Experimental Planning / Active Learning

Uncertainty quantification (UQ) is integral to BO's ability to balance exploration and exploitation. In materials design, mixed-variable problems (combining continuous variables such as temperature with categorical variables such as element choice) require specialized UQ-aware models (zhang2022uncertaintyawaremixedvariablemachine pages 11-13, zhang2022uncertaintyawaremixedvariablemachine pages 1-2). Zhang et al. systematically compared frequentist (random forest-based Lolo) and Bayesian (latent variable Gaussian process) approaches, providing practical guidance that LVGP models are preferred for low-dimensional problems while random forests excel in high-dimensional, complex settings (zhang2022uncertaintyawaremixedvariablemachine pages 11-13). Qian et al. introduced a knowledge-driven framework combining Bayesian model averaging with optimal experimental design under uncertainty, enabling simultaneous surrogate model learning and adaptive exploration of materials design spaces (qian2023knowledgedrivenlearningoptimization pages 8-10, arroyave2022aperspectiveon pages 3-6). Future directions include dynamic feature selection during optimization campaigns and physics-informed uncertainty models.

**Key references:**
- Zhang, H. *et al.* "Uncertainty-Aware Mixed-Variable Machine Learning for Materials Design," *Research Square* (2022). DOI: [10.21203/rs.3.rs-1987975/v1](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-1987975/v1)
- Qian, X. *et al.* "Knowledge-driven learning, optimization, and experimental design under uncertainty for materials discovery," *Patterns* **4**, 100863 (2023). DOI: [10.1016/j.patter.2023.100863](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2023.100863)

## 4. Foundation-Model- and Large-Language-Model-Assisted BO

The integration of large language models (LLMs) with BO represents a rapidly evolving frontier. Kristiadi et al. rigorously examined whether LLMs are genuinely useful for principled BO over molecules, finding that LLMs can serve as effective feature extractors for GP-based surrogates, but only when pre-trained or fine-tuned with domain-specific chemical data (kristiadi2024asoberlook pages 1-2, kristiadi2024asoberlook pages 9-10). Ranković and Schwaller introduced GOLLuM, which reframes LLM fine-tuning as GP marginal likelihood optimization through deep kernel learning, jointly optimizing LLM representations and GP hyperparameters (rankovic2504gollumgaussianprocess pages 2-4, rankovic2504gollumgaussianprocess pages 1-2). Applied to Buchwald–Hartwig reaction optimization, GOLLuM nearly doubled the discovery rate of high-performing reactions (from 24% to 43% coverage of top-5% reactions in 50 iterations) compared to static LLM embeddings (rankovic2504gollumgaussianprocess pages 1-2). The approach implicitly performs contrastive learning, yielding better-structured embedding spaces and improved uncertainty calibration across 19 chemistry benchmarks (rankovic2504gollumgaussianprocess pages 10-13). Other notable work includes LLAMBO, which frames BO as a natural language problem for zero-shot warm-starting (akke2025bayesianoptimizationfor pages 2-3).

**Key references:**
- Kristiadi, A. *et al.* "A Sober Look at LLMs for Material Discovery: Are They Actually Good for Bayesian Optimization Over Molecules?" *arXiv* (2024). DOI: [10.48550/arxiv.2402.05015](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2402.05015)
- Ranković, B. & Schwaller, P. "GOLLuM: Gaussian Process Optimized LLMs — Reframing LLM Finetuning through Bayesian Optimization," *arXiv* (2025). DOI: [10.48550/arxiv.2504.06265](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2504.06265)

## 5. Preference-Based / Human-in-the-Loop BO

Many materials optimization objectives involve subjective or difficult-to-quantify qualities where human expert judgment remains essential. Adachi et al. developed CoExBO (Collaborative and Explainable Bayesian Optimization), which integrates human expertise through preference learning—users compare candidate pairs rather than specifying explicit knowledge models—while providing Shapley-value-based explanations to build trust (adachi2023loopinginthe pages 3-5, adachi2023loopinginthe pages 1-2, adachi2023loopinginthe pages 2-3). Critically, CoExBO includes a no-harm guarantee: even with adversarial human input, the algorithm converges to vanilla BO asymptotically (adachi2023loopinginthe pages 1-2). The framework was validated on lithium-ion battery electrolyte design, demonstrating significant speedup over baseline methods (adachi2023loopinginthe pages 7-9, adachi2023loopinginthe pages 22-24). Deneault et al. extended preferential BO to 3D printing, optimizing subjective qualities such as surface finish and fine detail clarity that resist straightforward quantification (adachi2023loopinginthe pages 9-10). Future opportunities include multi-expert aggregation and integration with LLM-based preference elicitation.

**Key references:**
- Adachi, M. *et al.* "Looping in the Human: Collaborative and Explainable Bayesian Optimization," *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems* (2024). DOI: [10.48550/arxiv.2310.17273](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2310.17273)
- Deneault, J. R. *et al.* "Preferential Bayesian Optimization Improves the Efficiency of Printing Objects with Subjective Qualities," *Digital Discovery* (2025). DOI: [10.1039/d4dd00320a](https://doi.org/10.1039/d4dd00320a)

## 6. Multi-Objective Materials Design

Real-world materials design invariably involves balancing multiple competing objectives (e.g., strength vs. ductility, optical properties vs. cost). Low et al. introduced Evolution-Guided Bayesian Optimization (EGBO), which integrates evolutionary selection pressure in parallel with the q-Noisy Expected Hypervolume Improvement (qNEHVI) acquisition function (low2024evolutionguidedbayesianoptimization pages 3-4, low2024evolutionguidedbayesianoptimization pages 1-2). EGBO was demonstrated on a self-driving lab for seed-mediated silver nanoparticle synthesis targeting three objectives with complex constraints, achieving better Pareto front coverage and more feasible solution proposals than standard qNEHVI (low2024evolutionguidedbayesianoptimization pages 5-7, low2024evolutionguidedbayesianoptimization pages 2-3). Khatamsaz et al. developed an entropy-based constrained multi-objective BO framework that actively learns unknown design constraints, identifying 21 Pareto-optimal alloys in the Mo–Nb–Ti–V–W system for gas turbine applications (wu2024racetothe pages 11-11). Future challenges include scaling to many objectives (>3), handling mixed categorical-continuous parameter spaces, and developing batch-parallel multi-objective strategies for high-throughput platforms.

**Key references:**
- Low, A. K. Y. *et al.* "Evolution-guided Bayesian optimization for constrained multi-objective optimization in self-driving labs," *npj Computational Materials* **10**, 1–11 (2024). DOI: [10.1038/s41524-024-01274-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-024-01274-x)
- Khatamsaz, D. *et al.* "Bayesian optimization with active learning of design constraints using an entropy-based approach," *npj Computational Materials* **9**, 1–14 (2023). DOI: [10.1038/s41524-023-01006-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-023-01006-7)

## 7. Robust BO Under Noisy Experimental Data

Experimental noise from instrumentation imprecision and uncontrolled environmental factors can undermine BO performance. Aldeghi et al. introduced Golem, an algorithm-agnostic framework that identifies solutions robust to input uncertainty by optimizing a robust objective function g(x) = E[f(x̃)] over the noise distribution (aldeghi2021golemanalgorithm pages 3-4, aldeghi2021golemanalgorithm pages 4-5, aldeghi2021golemanalgorithm pages 1-2). Golem uses tree-based surrogate models enabling analytical computation of the robust objective for any parametric probability distribution, supporting continuous, discrete, and categorical variables (aldeghi2021golemanalgorithm pages 4-5). It was validated on HPLC protocol optimization under significant noise, demonstrating that robust optima can differ substantially from nominal optima (aldeghi2021golemanalgorithm pages 13-15, aldeghi2021golemanalgorithm pages 33-35). Diwale et al. presented an augmented BO approach specifically targeting non-Gaussian, non-sub-Gaussian noise processes common in material discovery, characterizing convergence behavior under challenging noise distributions (aldeghi2021golemanalgorithm pages 8-9). Key future opportunities include developing ML models to recover noiseless objective functions from noisy data and integrating robust optimization with multi-objective and constrained settings.

**Key references:**
- Aldeghi, M. *et al.* "Golem: an algorithm for robust experiment and process optimization," *Chemical Science* **12**, 14792–14807 (2021). DOI: [10.1039/d1sc01545a](https://doi.org/10.1039/d1sc01545a)
- Diwale, S. *et al.* "Bayesian Optimization for Material Discovery Processes with Noise," *Molecular Systems Design & Engineering* **7**, 622–636 (2022). DOI: [10.1039/d1me00154j](https://doi.org/10.1039/d1me00154j)

## 8. Community Benchmark Development for BO

Standardized benchmarks are essential for fair comparison of BO algorithms across chemistry and materials domains. Liang et al. benchmarked surrogate model and acquisition function combinations across five diverse experimental materials systems, finding that Gaussian processes with anisotropic kernels and random forests show comparable, robust performance (baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 11-12). Hickman et al. expanded the Olympus benchmarking platform to 33 datasets from chemistry and materials science literature, covering chemical reaction optimization, materials manufacturing, and redox-active materials design, with support for mixed (continuous, discrete, categorical) parameters and multiple objectives (hickman2023olympusenhancedbenchmarking pages 7-9). The 2024 Bayesian Optimization Hackathon for Chemistry and Materials, organized by the Acceleration Consortium and Merck KGaA with over 100 participants from 19 countries, produced new benchmarks including ScattBO (simulating an SDL for materials discovery), curated resource lists, and adapted test surfaces for generalized BO research (baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 11-12, baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 16-17, baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 2-3). Future needs include benchmarks reflecting real-world experimental noise, constraint handling, and multi-fidelity scenarios.

**Key references:**
- Liang, Q. *et al.* "Benchmarking the performance of Bayesian optimization across multiple experimental materials science domains," *npj Computational Materials* **7** (2021). DOI: [10.1038/s41524-021-00656-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-021-00656-9)
- Hickman, R. *et al.* "Olympus, enhanced: benchmarking mixed-parameter and multi-objective optimization in chemistry and materials science," *ChemRxiv* (2023). DOI: [10.26434/chemrxiv-2023-74w8d](https://doi.org/10.26434/chemrxiv-2023-74w8d)

## 9. Domain-Specific BO Tools for Synthesis and Processing Optimization

Accessible, chemistry-tailored software is critical for broader BO adoption. Shields et al. introduced EDBO (Experimental Design via Bayesian Optimization), a flexible Python package that enables chemists to apply BO to reaction optimization with both continuous and categorical variables, demonstrating that BO outperforms human experts in optimization efficiency for palladium-catalyzed reactions, Mitsunobu reactions, and deoxyfluorination (shields2021bayesianreactionoptimization pages 1-2, shields2021bayesianreactionoptimization pages 2-3, shen2025bayesianoptimizationfor pages 10-11). Hickman et al. developed Atlas, an application-agnostic Python library specifically tailored to SDL needs, providing unified access to mixed-parameter, multi-objective, constrained, robust, multi-fidelity, meta-learning, and molecular optimization algorithms in a single tool built on PyTorch, GPyTorch, and BoTorch (hickman2025atlasabrain pages 1-3, hickman2025atlasabrain pages 3-5, hickman2025atlasabrain pages 25-28). Other emerging tools include NEXTorch, which extends BoTorch for chemical engineering applications with intuitive interfaces (wang2021nextorchadesign pages 1-2), and Web-BO, a GUI-based platform linking BO to electronic lab notebooks for code-free usage. Future opportunities include further democratization through web interfaces, standardized API integration with laboratory equipment, and interoperability across SDL platforms (shen2025bayesianoptimizationfor pages 19-21).
