## Literature Review: Bayesian Optimization for Chemistry and Materials Science — Benchmarks, Software, Education, and Reviewer Recommendations

### 1. Overview of the Manuscript and Its Context

The manuscript titled "Bayesian Optimization Hackathon for Chemistry and Materials" (Baird et al., 2025) describes a 2-day virtual hackathon co-hosted by the Acceleration Consortium (University of Toronto) and Merck KGaA on March 27–28, 2024. With over 100 participants across 69 organizations in 19 countries, the event produced numerous projects spanning algorithm development, benchmark creation, instructional tutorials, and real-world applications of Bayesian optimization (BO) in the physical sciences (baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 1-2, baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 3-6). The hackathon's outputs—including benchmarking tools such as ScattBO, tutorial notebooks (e.g., for Gauche, Ax, and BayBE), retrieval-augmented BO pipelines, and curated "Awesome Lists" of BO resources—exemplify a community-driven approach to accelerating BO research in chemistry and materials (baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 9-10, baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 7-9, baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 11-12, baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 16-17).

### 2. Bayesian Optimization Algorithms and Software for Chemistry

The foundational BO algorithms developed specifically for chemistry include **Phoenics**, a Bayesian optimizer combining BO with kernel density estimation (Häse et al., *ACS Central Science*, 2018), and **Gryffin**, which extends BO to categorical variables informed by expert knowledge (Häse et al., 2021) (shen2025bayesianoptimizationfor pages 6-8, hase2021gryffinanalgorithm pages 35-35). The same group developed **Golem** for robust experiment optimization (Aldeghi et al., *Chemical Science*, 2021) and **Atlas**, a comprehensive BO library tailored for self-driving laboratories (Hickman et al., *Digital Discovery*, 2025) (hickman2025atlasabrain pages 29-31, hickman2025atlasabrain pages 31-33). Additional domain-specific software includes **BayBE** (Bayesian Back End) from Merck KGaA for experimental planning in low-data regimes (Fitzner et al., *Digital Discovery*, 2025), **BoFire** (Bayesian Optimization Framework Intended for Real Experiments; Dürholt et al., 2025), **NEXTorch** built on BoTorch for chemical engineering (Wang et al., *JCIM*, 2021), **Web-BO** providing a GUI-based interface for chemistry BO (Mroz et al., *Faraday Discussions*, 2025), and **ProcessOptimizer** for accessible BO in process optimization (Bertelsen et al., *JCIM*, 2025). The general-purpose **BoTorch** framework (Balandat et al., 2020) serves as the backbone for many of these chemistry-specific tools (hickman2025atlasabrain pages 29-31).

### 3. Community Benchmarks

Benchmarking efforts are central to the field. The **Olympus** framework (Häse et al., *Mach. Learn.: Sci. Technol.*, 2021) introduced experimentally derived benchmark datasets for evaluating optimization planners (hase2021gryffinanalgorithm pages 35-35, hickman2022bayesianoptimizationwith pages 27-28), later extended in **Olympus, enhanced** to handle mixed-parameter and multi-objective problems with 33 datasets across chemistry and materials science (Hickman et al., 2023) (hickman2025atlasabrain pages 31-33). The landmark study by Liang et al. (2021) benchmarked BO across five diverse experimental materials domains, finding that Gaussian processes with anisotropic kernels and random forests provide robust performance (liang2021benchmarkingtheperformance pages 18-21). Sabanza-Gil et al. (2025) provided best practices for multi-fidelity BO in molecular and materials research (shen2025bayesianoptimizationfor pages 6-8), while Wu et al. (2024) evaluated BO against grid search on chemical problems (baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 7-9).

### 4. Self-Driving Laboratories and the Acceleration Consortium

BO is a key decision-making component of self-driving laboratories (SDLs). Tom et al. (2024) provided a comprehensive Chemical Reviews overview of SDLs covering hardware, software, and applications across chemistry and materials science (baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 1-2). Abolhasani and Kumacheva (2023) surveyed the rise of SDLs in chemical and materials sciences. The Acceleration Consortium at the University of Toronto has been a central node in this ecosystem, supporting initiatives like the hackathon and driving the development of tools such as Atlas and ChemOS (baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 1-2). The "frugal twin" concept for low-cost SDLs (Lo et al., *Digital Discovery*, 2024) and community perspectives on autonomous experimentation (Stach et al., *Matter*, 2021) further contextualize the need for accessible BO infrastructure.

### 5. Educational Efforts and Hackathons

The BO hackathon builds on a growing tradition of community-driven hackathons in chemistry and materials. Jablonka et al. (2023) reported 14 projects from an LLM hackathon for materials science and chemistry (baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 3-6), followed by a second LLM hackathon documented by Zimmermann et al. (2024). Mannodi-Kanakkithodi et al. (2023) described a framework for materials informatics education through MRS workshops. The BO hackathon itself produced tutorials for BayBE, Gauche, Ax, and other tools; benchmark tasks released on Hugging Face; and a curated "Awesome List" of BO resources for chemistry (baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 9-10, baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 13-14, baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 16-17). The landmark Shields et al. (2021) paper in *Nature*, which demonstrated BO for reaction optimization, has also served as an important educational touchstone for the community (liang2021benchmarkingtheperformance pages 18-21).
