### Category 1: Works that explicitly arose from or acknowledge the AC BO Hackathon

**1. Official hackathon proceedings preprint**

Sterling G. Baird, Mehrad Ansari, Zartashia Afzal, Qianxiang Ai, Alexander Al-Feghali, Mathieu Alain, Matias Altamirano, Thomas Andrews, Andy Sode Anker, … [100+ co-authors] … Yunheng Zou, "Bayesian Optimization Hackathon for Chemistry and Materials," ChemRxiv, June 2025. DOI: 10.26434/chemrxiv-2025-dzh5z.

This preprint is the primary document reporting the hackathon itself. It describes all 45 team projects submitted during the event, with detailed summaries of each project's methodology and findings. It covers hackathon logistics, participant demographics (139 active participants from 39 teams across 52 academic institutions, 3 national labs, and 14 companies), and references the central project repository at https://ac-bo-hackathon.github.io/ (baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 2-3, baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 3-6). The acknowledgements state that the work was supported by funding to the University of Toronto's Acceleration Consortium from the Canada First Research Excellence Fund (baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 16-17).

**2. Ranking over Regression for Bayesian Optimization and Molecule Selection**

Gary Tom, Stanley Lo, Samantha Corapi, Alan Aspuru-Guzik, and Benjamin Sanchez-Lengeling, "Ranking over Regression for Bayesian Optimization and Molecule Selection," arXiv:2410.09290 (October 2024); published in *APL Machine Learning* 3, 036113 (2025).

This paper introduces Rank-based Bayesian Optimization (RBO), which uses a ranking surrogate model instead of a regression model for molecular selection. The work demonstrates that ranking models can match or outperform conventional BO surrogates, particularly for datasets with rough structure-property landscapes and activity cliffs (tom2025rankingoverregression pages 7-10). The exact acknowledgement sentence reads: **"This project was also part of the Acceleration Consortium Bayesian optimization hackathon."** (tom2025rankingoverregression pages 7-10). This work corresponds to hackathon Project 44 ("Rank-based Bayesian Optimization"), as described in the hackathon proceedings paper (baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 15-16).

### Summary

The systematic search identified exactly **two works** that explicitly arose from or acknowledge the AC BO Hackathon: the official hackathon proceedings preprint (Baird et al., ChemRxiv, 2025) and one peer-reviewed journal paper (Tom et al., *APL Machine Learning*, 2025). No additional published papers, preprints, software release papers, dataset papers, or benchmark papers were found in the searched literature corpus to explicitly acknowledge, cite, or reference the hackathon by name or URL. The hackathon proceedings document that it produced 45 team projects with associated GitHub repositories archived at https://ac-bo-hackathon.github.io/, but the downstream formal publication record that explicitly credits the event is limited to the Tom et al. paper at this time.
