## Post-Hackathon Uptake of Research Artifacts from the AC BO Hackathon 2024

### Background

The Acceleration Consortium Bayesian Optimization Hackathon for Chemistry and Materials (AC BO Hackathon 2024) was a two-day virtual event organized by the Acceleration Consortium (University of Toronto) and Merck KGaA, bringing together over 100 participants across 69 organizations in 19 countries to develop Bayesian optimization tools for the physical sciences (baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 6-7, baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 2-3). The hackathon produced 45 team projects, documented in a comprehensive overview preprint by Baird et al. (2025) on ChemRxiv (baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 6-7, baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 3-6). The following analysis examines the downstream literature uptake of seven specific artifacts from this event.

### Detailed Findings by Artifact

#### (a) ScattBO — Benchmark for BO of materials discovery from scattering data (Andy S. Anker)

ScattBO is documented as Project 24 in the hackathon overview, described as "a Python-based benchmark that simulates a self-driving laboratory (SDL) for materials discovery" using scattering data (baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 11-12). Despite thorough searching, no independent peer-reviewed or preprint publications were found that explicitly cite or benchmark against ScattBO. A potentially related follow-up by Anker et al. (2026) on "Autonomous synthesis of nanoparticles with target scattering patterns" in *ACS Nano* was identified but could not be accessed to verify whether it cites ScattBO directly. **ScattBO currently has no documented external literature uptake.**

#### (b) GAUCHE Library Tutorial (Ryan-Rhys Griffiths, Leo Klarner)

The GAUCHE tutorial (hackathon Project 35) introduced Gaussian process kernels for discrete molecular representations within the GAUCHE library, originally published at NeurIPS 2023 (baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 13-14, baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 14-15). The hackathon tutorial itself—as a distinct artifact—has no independently traceable citations. However, the underlying GAUCHE library is widely cited in the recent BO literature: it appears in Stuckner et al. (2026) on benchmarking BO frameworks (stuckner2026benchmarkingbayesianoptimization pages 24-26), in Mroz et al. (2025) on Web-BO for accessible chemistry BO (mroz2025webbotowardsincreased pages 12-14, mroz2025webbotowardsincreased pages 2-5), and in Li et al. (2024) on constrained multi-objective BO (li2411constrainedmultiobjectivebayesian pages 11-12). **The tutorial's impact is subsumed into the broader GAUCHE library citations; the tutorial itself has no documented independent uptake.**

#### (c) RAMBO — Retrieval-Augmented Initialization for BO of Chemical Reactions (Schwaller group, EPFL)

RAMBO is described as hackathon Project 41, combining "retrieval-augmented generation with Bayesian optimization to enhance the initial point selection for new optimization tasks," demonstrated on Suzuki–Miyaura coupling reactions (baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 16-17). No independent peer-reviewed or preprint publications were found that explicitly cite RAMBO by name. The same research group (Ranković and Schwaller) published GOLLuM (2025), which extends the theme of LLM-augmented Bayesian optimization for chemical reactions but does not explicitly cite RAMBO in the examined pages (baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 15-16). **RAMBO currently has no documented external literature uptake**, though the thematic line of work continues actively within the Schwaller group.

#### (d) BayBE — Bayesian Back End (Merck KGaA; Martin Fitzner, Adrian Šošić, Alexander V. Hopp)

BayBE is the most successful artifact from the hackathon in terms of documented uptake. The hackathon project (Project 7) used BayBE for corrosion inhibitor optimization (baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 9-10). The full BayBE paper was published in *Digital Discovery* in 2025 and reports 34 citations (fitzner2025baybeabayesian pages 1-2, fitzner2025baybeabayesian pages 7-8, fitzner2025baybeabayesian pages 2-3). Confirmed citing and using works include:

1. **Nazeri et al. (2026)**, "RAISE: a self-driving laboratory for interfacial property formulation discovery," *Digital Discovery* 5:2254–2270 — integrates BayBE as the BO backend in a closed-loop self-driving laboratory for surface wettability optimization (nazeri2026raiseaselfdriving pages 6-10).
2. **Stuckner et al. (2026)**, "Benchmarking Bayesian Optimization Frameworks and Acquisition Strategies for Materials Discovery and Autonomous Laboratories" — benchmarks BayBE against Ax, Lolopy, and Hyperopt; finds "BayBE GP in combination with EI was found to be the optimum or near-optimum choice across all benchmarks" (stuckner2026benchmarkingbayesianoptimization pages 24-26, stuckner2026benchmarkingbayesianoptimization pages 1-6).
3. **Mroz et al. (2025)**, "Web-BO: towards increased accessibility of Bayesian optimisation (BO) for chemistry," *Faraday Discussions* 256:221–234 — cites BayBE as software released by Merck to support BO in chemistry (mroz2025webbotowardsincreased pages 12-14, mroz2025webbotowardsincreased pages 2-5).
4. **Wang et al. (2025)**, "Advanced Experiment Design Strategies for Drug Development," *Advanced Intelligent Discovery* 2 — lists BayBE as a key BO tool for experiment design (wang2025advancedexperimentdesign pages 10-11).
5. **Dürholt et al. (2025)**, "BoFire: Bayesian Optimization Framework Intended for Real Experiments," arXiv — references BayBE in its framework comparison (durholt2025bofirebayesianoptimization pages 3-6).
6. **Soni et al. (2026)**, "Accelerated Development of Energy Materials through Automation and Artificial Intelligence," ChemRxiv — cites BayBE documentation (soni2025accelerateddevelopmentof pages 30-31).

**BayBE has strong, well-documented external uptake** with extensive citations, direct use in self-driving laboratories, and benchmarking evaluations.

#### (e) MolDAIS — Molecular Descriptor Selection (Ohio State; Farshud Sorourifar, Joel A. Paulson)

MolDAIS originated as hackathon Project 21 ("Benchmarking Molecular Descriptors with Actively Identified Subsets") (baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 11-12) and matured into a peer-reviewed publication: Sorourifar, Banker & Paulson (2025), "Adaptive subspace Bayesian optimization over molecular descriptor libraries for data-efficient chemical design," *Digital Discovery* 4(10):2910–2926, which reports 7 citations (sorourifar2025adaptivesubspacebayesian pages 13-15, sorourifar2025adaptivesubspacebayesian pages 1-2, sorourifar2025adaptivesubspacebayesian pages 2-3). The method demonstrates that MolDAIS "consistently achieves the lowest median RMSE" across molecular property benchmarks and can find near-optimal candidates from libraries of over 100,000 molecules (sorourifar2025adaptivesubspacebayesian pages 8-9, sorourifar2025adaptivesubspacebayesian pages 9-11). A follow-on paper from the same group, Muthyala, Sorourifar et al. (2026), "Generative Multiobjective Bayesian Optimization with Scalable Batch Evaluations for Sample-Efficient De Novo Molecular Design," *Industrial & Engineering Chemistry Research* 65:628–642, extends the molecular optimization work from MolDAIS (muthyala2025generativemultiobjectivebayesian pages 51-54). **MolDAIS has documented uptake** through its maturation into a peer-reviewed publication and subsequent extension.

#### (f) Honegumi — Template Generator for BO Scripts (Sterling G. Baird)

Honegumi was published as a preprint by Baird, Falkowski & Sparks (2026), "Honegumi: An Interface for Accelerating the Adoption of Bayesian Optimization in the Experimental Sciences," arXiv:2502.06815, reporting 5 citations (baird2502honegumianinterface pages 5-7, baird2502honegumianinterface pages 1-3). Documented citations and uses include:

1. **Garay Ruiz et al. (2026)**, "Database utility for cyclovoltammetry knowledge (DUCK): unified platform for electrochemical data," *Digital Discovery* 5:1736–1745 — explicitly states that the authors implemented "a simple Bayesian optimization strategy, starting from the basic parameters of the Honegumi framework" for their electrochemical experiments (garayruiz2026databaseutilityfor pages 7-8).
2. **Sayeed, Soneji, Baird & Sparks (2025)**, "Honegumi RAG Assistant: an agentic system for accelerating Bayesian optimization adoption in experimental sciences," ChemRxiv (unobtainable) — directly extends Honegumi with a retrieval-augmented generation assistant.

**Honegumi has documented uptake** through direct use in published experimental workflows and explicit extension into an agentic system.

#### (g) "Awesome Bayesian Optimization" List (Ben Blaiszik, Materials Data Facility)

The "Bayesian Optimization Awesome List" is documented as hackathon Project 43, described as a curated collection of "software, tutorials, research papers, and other resources related to Bayesian optimization in materials science and chemistry" (baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 16-17). No independent peer-reviewed or preprint publications citing the awesome list were found. As a community curation resource hosted on GitHub, this artifact's impact is inherently difficult to track through traditional citation metrics. **The awesome list has no documented literature uptake** in the peer-reviewed or preprint record.

### Summary

Of the seven AC BO Hackathon 2024 artifacts examined, three demonstrate clear documented uptake in the peer-reviewed and preprint literature: **BayBE** (34 citations, extensive use and benchmarking), **MolDAIS** (matured into a peer-reviewed paper with 7 citations and follow-on work), and **Honegumi** (5 citations, direct use in published experimental workflows). The **GAUCHE tutorial** contributed to the visibility of the underlying GAUCHE library, which is widely cited, though the tutorial itself is not independently cited. Three artifacts — **ScattBO**, **RAMBO**, and the **awesome Bayesian optimization list** — have no documented independent uptake in the peer-reviewed or preprint literature as of the time of this analysis, though thematic continuations of RAMBO's ideas exist in the Schwaller group's subsequent publications.
