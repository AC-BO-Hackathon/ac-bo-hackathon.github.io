# Strengths and Limitations of Bayesian Optimization in Chemistry and Materials Science: A Literature Review of Scientific Lessons Learned

## 1. Introduction

Bayesian optimization (BO) has emerged as a central methodology for data-efficient experimental design in chemistry and materials science. By coupling probabilistic surrogate models—most commonly Gaussian processes (GPs)—with acquisition functions that balance exploration and exploitation, BO enables sequential or batched decision-making under resource constraints (desimpel2026bayesianoptimizationfor pages 3-4, guo2023bayesianoptimizationfor pages 1-2). This review synthesizes the demonstrated strengths and limitations of BO as documented in recent literature, organized to support a discussion of scientific lessons learned.

### 2.1 Performance in Low-Data and Data-Scarce Regimes

BO is expressly designed for settings where each experimental evaluation is expensive, and initial data are scarce (desimpel2026bayesianoptimizationfor pages 3-4, desimpel2026bayesianoptimizationfor pages 41-41). Shields et al. demonstrated that BO outperformed human decision-making in both average optimization efficiency and consistency for palladium-catalyzed direct arylation, Mitsunobu, and deoxyfluorination reactions (Shields, B. J. et al. *Nature* **590**, 89–96, 2021; DOI: 10.1038/s41586-021-03213-y) (shields2021bayesianreactionoptimization pages 1-2). Schilter et al. achieved greater than 80% conversion for four terminal alkyne substrates across two reaction routes in only 23 experiments, covering approximately 0.2% of the combinatorial space (Schilter, O. et al. *Chem. Sci.* **15**, 7732–7741, 2024; DOI: 10.1039/d3sc05607d) (schilter2024combiningbayesianoptimization pages 1-2). In materials domains, GP-based BO is most competitive in the low-data regimes typical of early-stage materials development, where neural networks and tree-structured Parzen estimators show poor performance (Stuckner, J. et al. 2026; DOI: 10.64631/zkvt6687) (stuckner2026benchmarkingbayesianoptimization pages 6-8). BO has been described as particularly beneficial "when experimental data are expensive, scarce, or challenging to obtain," with recent flow chemistry innovations maximizing information extracted per experiment (Desimpel, S. et al. *Chem. Soc. Rev.* **55**, 2731–2775, 2026; DOI: 10.1039/d5cs00962f) (desimpel2026bayesianoptimizationfor pages 41-41).

### 2.2 Multi-Objective Search

Many chemistry and materials problems require simultaneous optimization of competing properties. Agarwal et al. applied multi-objective BO (MOBO) to discover 22 Pareto-optimal energy-storage redoxmers from 1,400 molecules, at least 15 times more efficiently than brute-force or random selection (Agarwal, G. et al. *Chem. Mater.* **33**, 8133–8144, 2021; DOI: 10.1021/acs.chemmater.1c02040) (agarwal2021discoveryofenergy pages 1-4). Gopakumar et al. showed that the Maximin acquisition strategy was robust even when surrogate model accuracy was poor, across datasets of shape memory alloys, M₂AX phases, and piezoelectrics (Gopakumar, A. M. et al. *Sci. Rep.* **8**, 2018; DOI: 10.1038/s41598-018-21936-3) (gopakumar2018multiobjectiveoptimizationfor pages 2-3, gopakumar2018multiobjectiveoptimizationfor pages 1-2). Solomou et al. extended MOBO to precipitation-strengthened NiTi shape memory alloys using expected hypervolume improvement (EHVI) as the acquisition function (Solomou, A. et al. *Mater. Des.* **160**, 810–827, 2018; DOI: 10.1016/j.matdes.2018.10.014) (solomou2018multiobjectivebayesianmaterials pages 1-2). Khatamsaz et al. further demonstrated constrained multi-objective BO in gas turbine alloy design, identifying 21 Pareto-optimal alloys satisfying multiple constraints in the Mo-Nb-Ti-V-W system (Khatamsaz, D. et al. *npj Comput. Mater.* **9**, 1–14, 2023; DOI: 10.1038/s41524-023-01006-7) (khatamsaz2023bayesianoptimizationwith pages 1-2). However, Arróyave et al. noted that discovering the Pareto set becomes increasingly difficult as both the number of objectives and the input dimensionality increase (Arróyave, R. et al. *MRS Commun.* **12**, 1037–1049, 2022; DOI: 10.1557/s43579-022-00288-0) (arroyave2022aperspectiveon pages 7-9).

### 2.3 Transfer Learning and Warm-Starting

Transfer learning can substantially accelerate BO by leveraging data from related tasks. Shen et al. reviewed cases where multi-task BO enabled reaction optimization campaigns that achieved 10-fold productivity gains, such as the transfer of glucose datasets that reduced optimization of carbohydrate reactions from 96 to approximately 20 experiments (Shen, R. et al. *Processes* **13**, 2687, 2025; DOI: 10.3390/pr13092687) (shen2025bayesianoptimizationfor pages 11-14). The Bayesian Optimization Hackathon for Chemistry and Materials found that small percentages of transferred data (1–25%, especially 1–10%) improved optimization performance, whereas larger transfer sets (50–100%) sometimes degraded results relative to baseline (Baird, S. et al. *ChemRxiv*, 2025; DOI: 10.26434/chemrxiv-2025-dzh5z) (baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 13-14). This finding underscores both the promise and the risk of warm-starting: poorly matched or excessive transfer data can bias rather than accelerate the search.

### 2.4 Molecular and Materials Screening

BO is highly effective for screening large candidate libraries. Deshwal et al. searched a database of approximately 70,000 hypothetical covalent organic frameworks for methane deliverable capacity, identifying the best candidates while evaluating only ~110 structures and recovering 25% of the top 100 performers, outperforming random search, evolutionary algorithms, and one-shot supervised learning (Deshwal, A. et al. *Mol. Syst. Des. Eng.* **6**, 1066–1086, 2021; DOI: 10.33774/chemrxiv-2021-4624n) (deshwal2021bayesianoptimizationof pages 1-4). Schultz et al. integrated graph neural networks with multi-objective BO to screen MOFs for molecular separations (Schultz, L. E. et al. *J. Mater. Chem. A* **14**, 10836–10853, 2026; DOI: 10.1039/d5ta09133k) (schultz2026graphneuralnetworkbased pages 1-2). Dasetty et al. applied BO to screen 3,850 linear molecular probes for PFAS binding affinity (Dasetty, S. et al. *J. Chem. Eng. Data* **68**, 3148–3161, 2023; DOI: 10.1021/acs.jced.3c00404) (dasetty2023datadrivendiscoveryof pages 1-2). Wu et al. summarized BO-driven materials screening that achieved 15-fold efficiency improvements over random selection and 17-fold stability improvements in perovskite design while sampling only 1.8% of compositional space (Wu, Y. et al. *Digit. Discov.*, 2024; DOI: 10.1039/d3dd00234a) (wu2024racetothe pages 11-12).

### 2.5 Human-in-the-Loop Workflows

Rather than requiring full autonomy, BO integrates productively with domain expertise. Adams et al. showed that Bayesian priors encoding expert knowledge of likely phase regions and boundaries significantly improved autonomous materials phase mapping (Adams, F. et al. *Matter* **7**, 697–709, 2024; DOI: 10.1016/j.matt.2024.01.005) (adams2024humanintheloopforbayesian pages 1-3). Harris et al. introduced a Dual-GP approach that adds active quality control with flexible human intervention for autonomous experiments (Harris, S. B. et al. *npj Comput. Mater.* **11**, 2025; DOI: 10.1038/s41524-024-01485-2) (harris2025activeoversightand pages 1-5). Adachi et al. developed CoExBO, a collaborative and explainable BO framework with a no-harm guarantee that enables expert preference integration even when experts make mistakes, validated in lithium-ion battery design (Adachi, M. et al. *ArXiv*, 2024; DOI: 10.48550/arxiv.2310.17273) (adachi2023loopinginthe pages 1-2). Toolkits such as NEXTorch (Wang, Y. et al. *J. Chem. Inf. Model.* **61**, 5312–5319, 2021; DOI: 10.1021/acs.jcim.1c00637) and BoFire (Dürholt, J. P. et al. *ArXiv*, 2025; DOI: 10.48550/arxiv.2408.05040) were explicitly designed to support human-in-the-loop deployment in real experimental settings (wang2021nextorchadesign pages 1-2, durholt2025bofirebayesianoptimization pages 1-3).

### 3.1 Sensitivity to Molecular Representation and Featurization

BO performance depends critically on how molecules and materials are encoded. Kochi et al. demonstrated that both the completeness and compactness of feature representations influence BO efficiency, and that biased or suboptimal representations adversely impact search performance; they proposed Feature Adaptive BO (FABO), which dynamically adapts representations during optimization cycles (Kochi, M. R. et al. *Chem. Sci.*, 2025; DOI: 10.1039/d5sc00200a) (rajabikochi2025adaptiverepresentationof pages 1-3, rajabikochi2025adaptiverepresentationof pages 3-3). Sorourifar et al. found that traditional molecular representations (fingerprints, graphs, SMILES) struggle in low-data regimes due to high dimensionality and misalignment with property landscapes, motivating adaptive subspace approaches (Sorourifar, F. et al. *Digit. Discov.* **4**, 2910–2926, 2025; DOI: 10.1039/d5dd00188a) (sorourifar2025adaptivesubspacebayesian pages 1-2, sorourifar2025adaptivesubspacebayesian pages 3-4). Kiyohara and Kumagai noted that standard GPs cannot automatically generate descriptors, making feature engineering a major bottleneck, and proposed deep kernel learning to address this (Kiyohara, S. and Kumagai, Y. *J. Phys. Chem. Lett.* **16**, 5244–5251, 2025; DOI: 10.1021/acs.jpclett.5c00592) (kiyohara2025bayesianoptimizationwith pages 1-2). In high-dimensional molecular fingerprint spaces (2048 dimensions), the BO Hackathon found that Random Forests substantially outperformed GPs, which suffered from the curse of dimensionality (baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 11-12).

### 3.2 Kernel Choice

There is no universally optimal kernel. Xu et al. conducted a systematic investigation showing that the squared exponential (SE) kernel frequently fails in high-dimensional settings due to gradient vanishing caused by improper length-scale initialization, whereas Matérn kernels are substantially more robust and handle dimensions up to 1,003 (Xu, Z. et al. *ArXiv*, 2025; DOI: 10.48550/arxiv.2402.02746) (xu2402standardgaussianprocess pages 9-11, xu2402standardgaussianprocess pages 1-2). Liang et al. found that GP with anisotropic (ARD) kernels and Random Forests demonstrated comparable performance and both outperformed GP with isotropic kernels across five experimental materials science domains (Liang, Q. et al. *npj Comput. Mater.* **7**, 2021; DOI: 10.1038/s41524-021-00656-9) (liang2021benchmarkingtheperformance pages 6-9). Desimpel et al. described the SOBayesian framework, which evaluates multiple kernel families at each iteration and selects the best via cross-validation rather than relying on a fixed kernel (desimpel2026bayesianoptimizationfor pages 26-28). Binois and Wycoff noted more broadly that distance-dependent kernels become problematic in high dimensions, motivating dimension-reducing transformations (Binois, M. and Wycoff, N. *ACM Trans. Evol. Learn. Optim.* **2**, 1–26, 2022; DOI: 10.1145/3545611) (binois2022asurveyon pages 4-8).

### 3.3 Quality of Warm-Start Data

As noted above, the benefit of warm-starting depends on the relevance and amount of transferred data. The BO Hackathon demonstrated that using 50–100% of available transfer data did not necessarily improve results and sometimes performed worse than no-transfer baselines, indicating that distribution-mismatched or irrelevant warm-start data can bias the search (baird2025bayesianoptimizationhackathon pages 13-14). This implies practitioners must carefully assess the similarity between source and target tasks before applying transfer learning.

### 3.4 Noisy Observations

Standard BO assumptions of noise-free or homoscedastic Gaussian noise are frequently violated in real experiments (wang2023recentadvancesin pages 7-8). Noise makes the current best value uncertain and worsens surrogate fitting; as the signal-to-noise ratio decreases, optimization effort increases substantially (binois2022asurveyon pages 4-8). Aldeghi et al. introduced Golem specifically to address robustness, finding that when experimental queries are noisy, collected data do not correctly match control factors with outcomes, degrading surrogate approximations (Aldeghi, M. et al. *Chem. Sci.* **12**, 14792–14807, 2021; DOI: 10.48550/arxiv.2103.03716) (aldeghi2021golemanalgorithm pages 1-2, aldeghi2021golemanalgorithm pages 8-9). For multiphasic systems with high experimental noise, noise-resilient acquisition functions such as qNEHVI have been recommended (desimpel2026bayesianoptimizationfor pages 26-28). Yu et al. advocated robust frameworks such as HEBO that handle heteroscedastic noise and non-stationary functions commonly encountered in real laboratories (Yu, Z. et al. *ArXiv*, 2026; DOI: 10.48550/arxiv.2604.01328) (yu2026efficientandprincipled pages 63-64).

### 3.5 High-Dimensional Search Spaces

Classical GP-based BO is most effective in low- to moderate-dimensional search spaces, typically 10–20 variables (desimpel2026bayesianoptimizationfor pages 13-14). GP surrogates become increasingly unreliable as dimensionality grows because kernel distances lose informativeness, training becomes unstable, and acquisition function optimization becomes more difficult (binois2022asurveyon pages 4-8). Many high-dimensional BO methods rely on structural assumptions (low intrinsic dimensionality, additive decomposition) that may not hold for a given chemical system (xu2402standardgaussianprocess pages 9-11). Stuckner et al. found that neural networks and tree-structured Parzen estimators promoted for high-dimensional problems showed poor performance in the low-data regimes typical of early-stage materials development (stuckner2026benchmarkingbayesianoptimization pages 6-8).

### 3.6 Batch-Size Selection

Parallel experimentation introduces a fundamental tension: all points in a batch are proposed from the same posterior, so larger batches reduce sequential learning (guo2023bayesianoptimizationfor pages 5-6). Fromer et al. noted that batch acquisition functions are non-additive and combinatorial, making exact optimization intractable even for modest batch sizes; naive top-k selection produces homogeneous batches lacking exploration diversity (Fromer, J. C. et al. *J. Chem. Inf. Model.* **65**, 4808–4817, 2025; DOI: 10.1021/acs.jcim.5c00214) (fromer2025batchedbayesianoptimization pages 1-5). Under noisy conditions, there is an additional trade-off between testing more unique conditions and replicating fewer conditions more times to reduce variance.

### 3.7 Acquisition-Function Optimization

Stuckner et al. found that posterior-mean acquisition consistently underperformed expected improvement (EI) and probability of improvement due to entrapment in local optima, while EI provided the best balance of exploration and exploitation across benchmark problems (stuckner2026benchmarkingbayesianoptimization pages 1-6, stuckner2026benchmarkingbayesianoptimization pages 19-22, stuckner2026benchmarkingbayesianoptimization pages 24-26). Liang et al. found that the lower confidence bound with an equal exploration-exploitation balance (LCB₁) outperformed other LCB variants and EI, which tended toward excessively greedy decisions (liang2021benchmarkingtheperformance pages 6-9). These findings emphasize that acquisition function choice is problem-dependent and can substantially alter optimization trajectories.

### 3.8 Computational Overhead

GP regression scales cubically with the number of observations, becoming costly after hundreds of samples (aldeghi2021golemanalgorithm pages 3-4). Stuckner et al. found that computational times varied dramatically across frameworks, from milliseconds (TPE) to minutes (neural networks and random forests) per iteration (stuckner2026benchmarkingbayesianoptimization pages 1-6). Liang et al. reported computational complexity ratios of RF:GP:GP-ARD = 1:1.32:1.54 (liang2021benchmarkingtheperformance pages 11-14). Beyond the optimization core, industrial deployment requires substantial software engineering for data serialization, API integration, and compatibility with laboratory hardware (durholt2025bofirebayesianoptimization pages 1-3, yu2026efficientandprincipled pages 63-64).

### 3.9 Reproducibility of Benchmark Comparisons

Benchmark rankings are sensitive to random seeds, data splits, and problem class. Stuckner et al. found notable variability across seeds within each surrogate-acquisition combination and released benchmark code to support reproducibility (stuckner2026benchmarkingbayesianoptimization pages 1-6, stuckner2026benchmarkingbayesianoptimization pages 24-26). Liang et al. used 50 independent runs across 10 five-fold data splits for robustness but noted that absolute performance metrics varied across datasets due to differences in data collection methodology (grid sampling vs. BO-guided paths) (liang2021benchmarkingtheperformance pages 6-9, liang2021benchmarkingtheperformance pages 11-14). Desimpel et al. highlighted that experimental head-to-head validation of BO algorithms against alternatives is rarely performed because repeated independent campaigns on the same chemical system are prohibitively expensive (desimpel2026bayesianoptimizationfor pages 13-14). Zhang et al. recommended developing structured benchmarking frameworks that systematize optimization landscapes by their characteristics rather than assuming universal best performance (zhang2604bayesianoptimizationfor pages 20-21).

## 4. Framework Comparisons: Noisy vs. Low-Noise Data

The literature does not reveal a strong consensus that any single BO framework is universally superior for noisy versus low-noise data. Rather, several complementary strategies emerge. For noisy data, Golem provides algorithm-agnostic robust optimization by identifying solutions robust to input uncertainty, using tree-based surrogates that analytically compute expected performance over noise distributions (aldeghi2021golemanalgorithm pages 1-2, aldeghi2021golemanalgorithm pages 4-5). Noise-resilient acquisition functions such as qNEHVI are recommended for multi-objective problems with high experimental noise (desimpel2026bayesianoptimizationfor pages 26-28). HEBO has been advocated for handling heteroscedastic noise and non-stationarity common in real experiments (yu2026efficientandprincipled pages 63-64). For low-noise, deterministic evaluations, standard GP-based BO with EI remains the default recommendation according to benchmarking studies (stuckner2026benchmarkingbayesianoptimization pages 1-6, stuckner2026benchmarkingbayesianoptimization pages 6-8). Binois and Wycoff noted that small amounts of observation noise can even act as beneficial regularization for GP surrogate fitting, but performance degrades sharply as signal-to-noise ratio drops (binois2022asurveyon pages 4-8). Overall, the choice of framework depends on the noise structure, dimensionality, and variable types of the specific chemical problem.

## 5. Deployment of BO Tools in Real Experimental Settings

Deploying BO in real laboratories remains nontrivial despite substantial progress. BoFire was developed specifically to bridge the gap between academic BO implementations and industrial deployment, providing JSON-serializability, REST API support, and integration with both self-driving laboratories and human-in-the-loop setups (durholt2025bofirebayesianoptimization pages 1-3). NEXTorch extended BoTorch with intuitive interfaces for laboratory scientists, acknowledging that many existing BO tools were designed for ML researchers rather than experimentalists (wang2021nextorchadesign pages 1-2). Yu et al. emphasized that standard BO assumptions (stationarity, homoscedasticity) often fail in real labs, requiring domain-specific design space transformations and dimensionality reduction that are difficult for experimental scientists without ML expertise (yu2026efficientandprincipled pages 63-64). Practical barriers include the need for custom featurization pipelines, data preprocessing, and hardware integration (durholt2025bofirebayesianoptimization pages 1-3, yu2026efficientandprincipled pages 63-64). Pilon et al. recently demonstrated RoboChem-Flex, a low-cost self-driving laboratory platform integrating BO with human-in-the-loop configurations that validated across six diverse chemistry case studies (Pilon, S. et al. *ChemRxiv*, 2025; DOI: 10.26434/chemrxiv-2025-73xqf). The trend toward GUI-based interfaces (e.g., Web-BO; Mroz, A. M. et al. *Faraday Discuss.* **256**, 221–234, 2025; DOI: 10.1039/d4fd00109e) and integration with electronic lab notebooks signals growing attention to accessibility, but full automation of BO in diverse chemical workflows remains an active area of development.

## 6. Conclusions

Bayesian optimization has demonstrably accelerated molecular and materials discovery in low-data regimes, multi-objective design, transfer-learning settings, large-scale screening campaigns, and human-in-the-loop experimental workflows. However, its effectiveness is contingent on appropriate choices of molecular representation, kernel, acquisition function, and batch strategy—choices for which no universal defaults exist. Sensitivity to these decisions, combined with challenges in handling high-dimensional spaces, noisy observations, computational scaling, and the scarcity of head-to-head experimental benchmarks, means that practitioners must carefully match BO configurations to the specific characteristics of their chemical system. The growing ecosystem of domain-oriented BO toolkits and benchmarking initiatives is beginning to address the deployment gap, but substantial work remains to make BO fully turnkey for experimental chemists and materials scientists.
